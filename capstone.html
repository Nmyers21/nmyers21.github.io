WGU D833 / Cybersecurity capstone / Academic lab

Ransomware response.
Recovery with a plan.

An investigation and remediation case study from the simulated NorthSound Health Systems environment, followed by a strategic plan to reduce recurrence and strengthen recovery.

Download writeup ↓
ContextHealthcare ransomware simulation
My roleInvestigation, containment & recovery
ToolsElastic / Kibana, pfSense, Windows Server Backup

01 / The problem

Shared storage unavailable.
Business operations disrupted.

The lab scenario began with inaccessible or altered files on a shared volume hosted by DC01. Departmental users could not reliably open or save files, and billing staff could not access claim data. My objective was to establish scope, contain the observed threat, restore service, and document the remaining risks.

02 / Investigation

Correlating evidence across systems.

I reviewed incident tickets and correlated Elastic/Kibana web-server, firewall, and ransomware-related logs. Suspicious web requests preceded encryption indicators on DC01, supporting a possible web-origin attack path.

The evidence supported a likely sequence of events, but did not establish the exact vulnerable component, exploit, or lateral-movement mechanism.

I compared alternate hypotheses and preserved the distinction between observed evidence and inference. The investigation also left data exposure unresolved; the collected evidence did not establish exfiltration.

03 / Response & recovery

Contain first. Then validate recovery.

  1. Block the observed communication path

    Applied bidirectional pfSense rules for the observed attacker address, enabled packet logging, and cleared active firewall states.

  2. Restore the affected shared volume

    Used Windows Server Backup to restore the shared volume from a known-good backup after containment.

  3. Verify service restoration

    Checked restored folders and readable files. The lab’s final ticket confirmed that users could access and save data normally.

04 / Strategic improvement

Translate incident lessons into priorities.

The follow-on proposal focused on two residual risks: recurrence through an unresolved public-facing web weakness and failure of future backup recovery.

Web security & vulnerability management

Proposed vulnerability scanning, patch verification, secure configuration review, WAF or reverse-proxy protections, centralized logs, and tracked remediation.

Ransomware-resilient recovery

Proposed immutable or offline copies, isolated backup credentials, monitored jobs, agreed recovery targets, and scheduled restore testing.

The plan included a proposed 12-week sequence for design, testing, deployment, and monitoring, with stakeholder ownership, implementation risks, and maintenance activities. These controls were recommendations, not completed production deployments.

05 / Takeaways

Recovery is a milestone.
Resilience is ongoing work.

The lab demonstrated immediate containment and successful service restoration. It also showed why a successful restore does not resolve an unknown entry point or prove that data was never accessed. My deliverables connected technical findings, response decisions, residual risk, and practical recommendations.
